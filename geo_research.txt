Research whether there are recurring, evidence-backed unmet problems worldwide
in workflows that depend on geographic coordinates, latitude/longitude, precise
locations, geospatial points, or location identifiers.

Do not begin by assuming that a map application, coordinate converter, GIS tool,
mobile app, API, developer tool, or SaaS product is needed.

The goal is to discover whether people, businesses, developers, field operators,
travelers, researchers, logistics teams, emergency workers, surveyors, property
professionals, agriculture users, outdoor users, or other operators repeatedly
experience friction when working with exact geographic locations.

Search globally and do not restrict the research to one country unless evidence
shows a problem is geography-specific.

Investigate public evidence from sources such as:
- Reddit discussions and complaints
- GIS / mapping / developer forums
- Stack Overflow and GIS Stack Exchange
- GitHub issues and feature requests
- product support forums
- mobile and web app reviews where accessible

Look broadly for recurring problems involving concepts such as:
latitude and longitude, coordinate capture, coordinate sharing, coordinate lookup,
reverse geocoding, geocoding, address-to-coordinate conversion, coordinate-to-address conversion,
GPS accuracy, location verification, coordinate validation, invalid or malformed coordinates,
different coordinate formats, decimal degrees, degrees/minutes/seconds, UTM, MGRS, plus codes,
geohashes, coordinate reference systems, CRS conversion, datum conversion, map projections,
copying coordinates between applications, bulk coordinate processing, CSV/Excel coordinate workflows,
location deduplication, nearby-point matching, distance calculation, route/location comparison,
geofencing, offline coordinate use, poor-connectivity environments, cross-border location formats,
places without conventional street addresses, remote/rural locations, field data collection,
photo/location metadata, location privacy, location audit/history, and location-based developer workflows.

Map strong problem candidates using the 14 workflow nodes:
Actor, Trigger, Input, Steps, Tools, Decisions, Handoffs, Delays, Rework, Errors, Output, Cost, Risk, Audit.

Cluster similar complaints into stable root-problem candidates instead of treating every feature request as a separate problem.
Distinguish problem evidence from solution demand.
